System integrator (PhD from an MIT affiliated Lab) with extensive experience in designing and optimizing resource restricted cloud and embedded platforms, involving substantial use of machine learning and mathematical modelling to capture physical-world behaviour and dynamics. Skilled in performance engineering, scalable architectures, and lifecycle management.

Overview

My work sits at the boundary of real-world sensing/actuation and production software, spanning embedded targets and cloud backends, where deterministic timing, measurable latency/throughput, and failure-safe behavior are first-order requirements. I build end-to-end systems—from low-level interfacing and drivers up through multi-stage perception and optimization pipelines—primarily in C/C++ and Python, with practical build/deployment stacks for constrained targets and integration pathways that support scalable operation.

Embedded perception and real-time delivery

In production embedded environments, I have architected and integrated QNX-based pipelines that fuse multi-modal camera streams to detect anomalous activities and unattended objects, while meeting strict real-time constraints. I have redesigned and optimized 3D perception stacks—object detection, pose estimation, and reconstruction—for resource-constrained deployment, including systematic tuning and evaluation with strong measured outcomes across detection and recognition tasks. A key part of my delivery has been building the “last mile” integration that makes these systems reliable: synchronized acquisition, predictable processing, and robust runtime behavior under embedded constraints.

Machine learning, modelling, and optimization

For industrial reliability and operational optimization, I have deployed, adapted, and fine-tuned anomaly detection approaches (including Isolation Forest, Autoencoders, Outlier Exposure, Deep SVDD, and LoOP), paired with comparative evaluation for robustness and performance. I have also designed and benchmarked a hybrid model predictive control (MPC) framework that combines first-principles dynamics with data-driven components to improve prediction fidelity and control responsiveness. On the systems side, I routinely drive performance engineering (multithreading, vectorization, priority tuning, buffer management, and low-level configuration).

Cloud and fleet-scale integration

I have delivered device-to-cloud and inter-device integration paths that support monitoring, data flow, and operational feedback loops, including CAN- and MQTT-based communication patterns where appropriate. My focus is on building reliable, observable interfaces between embedded systems and higher-level services, with attention to robustness, diagnostics, and lifecycle needs (deployment, updates, and validation). I validate integration and performance through hands-on debugging and instrumentation, including in-circuit tools when required.

Resource-constrained compute and deployment

I have deep experience building resource-aware real-time signal-processing and vision workloads in embedded C++, where correctness and determinism must hold under tight compute and memory limits. This includes real-time scheduling, multithreading, synchronization, and memory management for deterministic behavior; efficient 3D reconstruction and segmentation pipelines; and compact model integration using embedded runtimes such as TFLite/TVM, supported by benchmarking against optimized math and acceleration libraries.

Systems architecture and integration

My research foundation was developed in an MIT–SUTD lab environment, where I worked on component specification and system/software architecture in multi-team settings, with an emphasis on turning complex prototypes into cohesive systems. In that context, I built robust GPS protocol handling (e.g., NMEA/3GPP/UBP) and designed ROS nodes to integrate sensing, actuation, and control with efficient inter-node communication—skills that translate directly to modern embedded integration work.

Applied R&D across domains

Beyond core embedded delivery, I have built ML-forward systems that still demand “embedded-grade” discipline. Examples include embedded control software for additive manufacturing (G-code interpretation, motion/thermal control, and sensor interfacing) combined with ML-based parameter optimization, defect detection and correction, and maintenance forecasting; and portable medical imaging systems involving firmware for RF subsystems, synchronized real-time acquisition/buffering, and reconstruction algorithms optimized for computational efficiency. I have also delivered scalable neural retrieval and ranking pipelines (Spark + MongoDB ingestion, neural-symbolic retrieval, learn-to-rank evaluation with MAP/NDCG/Precision/MRR/latency) and clustering-based visualization for practical search workflows.

Imaging and embedded control foundations

Earlier in my career, I worked extensively on embedded imaging and microscopy systems, implementing real-time enhancement and reconstruction methods (deblurring/denoising/resolution improvement) in C++ on embedded devices, and building embedded vision features such as auto-focus, Z-stacking, stitching, segmentation, and spectral unmixing. This work was grounded in hardware-validated delivery— oscilloscope/logic-analyzer/JTAG debugging—and extended into embedded control software for sensor parameters, laser modulation, calibration routines, encoder integration, and closed-loop motor positioning.

Ongoing research collaboration

Alongside industry delivery, I continue technical-lead-level academic collaboration on system design and architecture using modern optimization and learning approaches, including graph neural networks and deep reinforcement learning (e.g., A3C), as well as dimensionality reduction and graph clustering methods used for system modularization.
